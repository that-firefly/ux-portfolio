BoilerJuice

Service design and UI for a more confident heating oil ordering experience

BoilerJuice is a UK heating oil price comparison service that helps customers find local deals. Unlike a mains-connected heating service, ordering heating oil requires customers to anticipate their needs, compare options and understand when their delivery will arrive.

The design challenge was to make this process feel straightforward, transparent and dependable, while supporting customers who may be ordering under pressure during colder months.

My focus was to simplify the end-to-end experience, reduce uncertainty and help customers complete their orders with greater confidence.

The challenge

Customers could discover that their heating oil was running low only when their tank was already critically low. For people living in remote locations, uncertainty around delivery could be particularly stressful, especially during winter.

The existing journey relied on phone orders and a basic online form. As demand grew, this created opportunities to make ordering more efficient and provide clearer information throughout the process.

The challenge was therefore more than updating the interface. It was about designing a service that made a time-sensitive household purchase easier to understand, navigate and manage.

Empathy mapping helped frame customer needs and opportunities for improvement.

My role and collaboration

Over 18 months, I held approximately 80% UX ownership within a cross-functional team of around 300 people. I collaborated with product owners, engineers, customer service specialists, heating oil partners and marketing professionals.

I contributed to the design and launch of a new service intended to make buying heating oil feel as effortless as being connected to a mains supply. My work covered new user journeys, legacy page redesigns and content for the Connected Service across the public website and mobile app.

I inherited parts of the original app design from another designer. While some elements evolved from that foundation, the current structure and layouts largely reflect my work.

Translating the service promise into design decisions

I approached the experience around three principles: clarity, reassurance and ease of use. These were design principles I used to address customer needs and service challenges, rather than formal brand guidelines.

01. Clarity. Help customers understand their options

Heating oil is a considered purchase, so customers need to understand their choices before committing. I redesigned the quote form to make obtaining a quote clearer and faster, and improved access to important information through an enhanced search function.

Design focus: clearer forms, information hierarchy and access to information.

02. Reassurance. Reduce uncertainty

When customers depend on a delivery to keep their homes warm, uncertainty can undermine confidence. I redesigned key checkout pages and improved the presentation of delivery expectations, aiming to make the process feel more transparent and trustworthy.

Design focus: checkout clarity, delivery information and visible next steps.

03. Ease of use. Make the next step obvious

I reviewed the end-to-end purchasing journey, including mobile use, to reduce unnecessary friction. Clearer flows, improved page layouts and subscription options supported both immediate ordering and ongoing customer needs.

Design focus: streamlined user flows, responsive layouts and repeat-use options.

Together, these decisions connected the practical needs of the service with the experience it needed to deliver: a straightforward way to compare, order and manage heating oil.

Process and approach

User research

I worked with the Product Manager on discovery, defining core problem statements and identifying opportunities through user feedback, research and iterative testing. The aim was to understand customer needs and turn those insights into clear design priorities.

My research and analysis included:

  • User interviews and ongoing feedback to understand customer needs, behaviours and pain points.
  • Google Analytics and behavioural analysis to investigate how people used the existing experience.
  • System Usability Scale (SUS) research to assess perceived usability.
  • Card sorting and information architecture mapping to improve how information was organised.
  • Accessibility audits to identify barriers and inform more inclusive design decisions.
  • Ecosystem maps, service blueprints, lean canvases, user flows and UX diaries to connect individual interactions with the wider service.

I used these methods to build an understanding of the wider service, identify potential pain points and help the team make informed decisions about what to improve.

Research and service design artifacts used to explore customer needs and the wider journey.

UI design: turning insights into interactions

I mapped user flows in Draw.io and Sketch, considering both successful and unsuccessful paths to identify potential issues before implementation. I created low- to mid-fidelity wireframes in Axure, Sketch, Figma and Balsamiq to explore layouts, clarify functionality and align stakeholders.

Quote form and subscription service

I redesigned the quote form to support a faster, clearer quoting experience and the subscription service. The focus was on making the process easier to follow and helping customers understand what information was required at each step.

Checkout and purchasing journey

I redesigned key checkout pages to reduce friction, improve transparency and create a more mobile-friendly purchasing journey. The design intent was to help customers move from comparing prices to completing an order with a clearer understanding of the process.

Search and information access

I also introduced an improved search function to make important documents and information easier to find, helping customers access relevant content without unnecessary effort.

My focus was not simply to modernise the interface. It was to make each step easier to understand, help customers make informed decisions and maintain confidence throughout the ordering journey.

Design handover and delivery

I partnered closely with developers to translate approved designs into the delivered experience. I provided guidelines, assets and documentation, and worked through regular check-ins and QA sessions to identify discrepancies and maintain design quality.

I also engaged stakeholders early, using research and testing to explain design decisions, agree priorities and balance user needs with business goals within an agile delivery process.

My contribution and outcomes

The work contributed to a more straightforward and transparent ordering experience.

Greater customer confidence

Clearer information and delivery expectations helped customers understand the process and feel more reassured when ordering.

More independent ordering

Customers were better able to complete orders with little or no support.

Customer service shift

Customer service calls shifted towards follow-ups rather than winter emergencies.

Support for repeat use

Alerts and subscription options gave customers more ways to manage their ongoing heating oil needs.

Operational resilience

Earlier reordering and clearer service information supported the business during periods of peak winter demand.

These outcomes reflect the qualitative impact described by the project. Where available, quantitative measures such as checkout completion, journey abandonment, support-call volume and subscription uptake would help demonstrate the scale of improvement.

Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of treating UX and UI as part of the service experience, rather than as a layer of visual polish. For BoilerJuice, the design needed to do more than look consistent: it needed to communicate clearly, reduce uncertainty and help customers complete an important household purchase with confidence.

By connecting research, service design and interface decisions, I helped shape an experience that addressed both customer needs and operational challenges.
